# PRD Quality Review — LittleSteps

## Overall verdict
The PRD has a clear, specific thesis: prepare caregivers before practice, keep the phone out of the way, log in therapists' units, and hand back a summary the therapist recognises. The glossary, guardrails and non-goals are unusually disciplined for a lean pilot PRD, and the IDs and cross-references are clean. The main risks are three things that will reach architecture and stories unresolved. Document import (FR-5) quietly contradicts the privacy guardrail that "AI requests contain only a nickname". The confirmed decision on reminders contradicts §8.2. Several AI guardrails ("never contradict a recorded restriction", "flagged") can't be tested because the data and mechanisms behind them are never defined. Done-ness is the weakest dimension. Everything else is adequate or strong for an invite-only pilot.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
§10 Confirmed Decisions states decisions as decisions, and the one `[NOTE FOR PM]` (§5 regulatory review) sits at a real tension. Trade-offs are mostly stated as what was chosen, not what was given up. For example, "v1 is a PWA" (§6) costs reliable iOS push, but that cost only shows up in addendum §B. "Reminders on by default" (FR-13) sits uneasily with the guilt-free tone, and the PRD never acknowledges this. The largest unacknowledged tension is between importing real therapy documents and the privacy guardrails (below).

### Findings
- **[high]** Document import contradicts the privacy guardrails (FR-5, §5, addendum §C "Report headers") — Real goal sheets and reports carry the child's name, date of birth, clinic and therapist names in their headers. FR-5 stores "the original document … privately" and uses AI to "propose draft Goals". §5 says "No names or identifiers are required anywhere" and "AI requests contain only a nickname or 'the child'". As written, both cannot hold, and architecture will have to choose without guidance. *Fix:* add an explicit decision to FR-5 and §5. Either (a) redact identifiers before extraction or storage, and decide whether that is automatic, done by the Caregiver (cropping or masking), or both, with a testable consequence such as "no identifier field from the header is sent to the AI provider"; or (b) state an explicit, scoped exception covering storage and AI processing of uploaded documents, including how long they are retained.
- **[medium]** Pilot data handling is deferred past the pilot (§5 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, §8.1) — The regulatory review is scoped to "before any public release", but the invite-only pilot already processes sensitive data about real children. The PRD doesn't cover pilot participant consent or terms, data retention after the pilot ends, or who on the operator side can access Family data. *Fix:* add 2–3 pilot-scoped guardrails to §5: consent at invite acceptance, retention or deletion when the pilot ends, and no operator access to Family content except for named support needs.
- **[low]** Confirmed decisions don't record what they give up (§10) — For example, "v1 is a PWA" gives up dependable iOS push and share-target import, and "one standard format" for Summaries gives up per-therapist templates. *Fix:* add a short "given up" clause to the 3–4 decisions that have real costs.

## Substance over theater — strong
The Vision is specific to this product: multi-professional goal fragmentation, coaching through prep rather than on screen, logging in therapist units, and "missed days never build up into a backlog". It couldn't be moved into another PRD in this category. Personas are unnamed composites that each drive features: the working parent drives Prep and the 20-second Review, the co-caregiver drives FR-1 attribution. The Tone guardrails ("No streaks, scores, or 'missed' counters") are carried into FR-10, FR-13 and FR-16 consequences, so they aren't decoration. The one piece of furniture is the Cost NFR.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Cost NFR has no bound (§6 "low running cost per Family, suitable for a small pilot") — Addendum §A asks architecture to weigh a "lighter stack" against this NFR, but there's no number to weigh against. AI import, AI suggestions and AI Coach Tips are the cost drivers. *Fix:* give a ceiling, e.g. "≤ $X per Family per month at pilot usage", or a total pilot budget.
- **[low]** UJ-1 overclaims learning (§2.3 UJ-1 Resolution, "the activity learns which tips work for this child") — The only mechanism specified is FR-12's "Helpful tips are shown first next time". "Learns" invites ML scope. *Fix:* reword to "tips the Caregiver marked helpful are shown first next time".
- **[low]** Some examples look lifted verbatim from specific source documents (§1 "Hold the book yourself and control the pace…", §3 Prompt Level ordering, UJ-2 "10 min → 15–20 min", §4.2 and addendum §C "Support X Learning" in title case, UJ-1 "Storybook together") — The PRD contains no names or PII and presents the product as for families generally. Still, a few idiosyncratic details, such as the unusual Prompt Level ordering and a capitalised goal title, read as transcribed from one family's paperwork rather than as generic examples. *Fix:* rephrase these as clearly generic examples, e.g. "a broad narrative goal such as 'support early learning'" and a standard prompt hierarchy.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is stated in §4.3: "The core problem it solves is the moment practice falls apart". It's reinforced by the prep, activity, review flow and by SM-5. A second thesis, consolidating goals from multiple Sources and producing therapist-legible Summaries, carries equal weight (§1, FR-3 to FR-7, FR-17, SM-3). The two are compatible, but the MVP treats all 17 FRs as equally in scope. That gives epics no sequencing logic for a small pilot. Counter-metrics exist, which is good, but the Success Metrics lack thresholds and measurement methods.

### Findings
- **[medium]** No priority or cut line inside the MVP (§8.1 "FR-1 to FR-17") — For an invite-only pilot, AI import (FR-5), AI suggestions (FR-9), Guided mode (FR-10), inventories (FR-15) and trend views (FR-16) are heavy, and some could follow a thinner first slice. Without priority, epics will follow ease rather than the thesis. *Fix:* tag FRs as pilot-critical (e.g. FR-1, 2, 4, 7, 10, 11, 12, 14, 17) or pilot-later (e.g. FR-5 AI extraction, FR-9, Guided mode, FR-15, FR-16 trends). This could stay inside v1 as sequencing only.
- **[medium]** Success Metrics lack thresholds and measurement (§9) — SM-1 doesn't say what share of pilot Families must hit "≥3 days a week for 8 consecutive weeks", or what counts as "use" (a Review logged? a Prep opened?). SM-3 ("therapists rate them as useful") has no collection method, and therapists aren't app users. SM-5 measures only tips a Caregiver bothered to rate, which biases it toward success. *Fix:* add a target share and a definition of an active day to SM-1. Add a method and bar to SM-3 (e.g. a short Caregiver-relayed or emailed rating, ≥4/5 from a majority). Define the SM-5 denominator as all tip displays after a Mark.
- **[low]** Counter-metric pairing is off (§9 SM-C2 "Session length … Counterbalances SM-5") — Session length counterbalances practice-volume pressure (SM-1, FR-10), not tip helpfulness. *Fix:* re-pair SM-C2 with SM-1, or explain the link to SM-5.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
Many FRs have good, testable consequences: FR-5 "Nothing becomes active until the Caregiver confirms it", FR-12 "Tips appear in under 2 seconds", FR-14 "≤30 seconds" and "edit history is kept", and FR-17 "AI never suggests a Goal status". The AI-safety guardrails, which carry the most product risk, are the least testable. Several FRs have one consequence or none that would let an engineer write acceptance criteria, and soft adjectives remain in places stories will copy from.

### Findings
- **[high]** AI guardrails depend on undefined data and mechanisms (FR-9 "never contradict a recorded restriction", FR-9 "Any suggestion that would change a therapeutic technique is flagged", §5 "Anything outside that scope is flagged") — No FR records a "restriction": it isn't a Glossary term, a Strategy field or a Child Profile field (unless "sensitivities" is meant). "Flagged" doesn't say by what or how it's tested, and "change a therapeutic technique" has no operational definition. These are the clinical-safety promises, and as written they can't be verified. *Fix:* define Restriction in the Glossary, either as a Strategy or Care Team note marked "avoid" or by naming Child Profile sensitivities explicitly, and make it a field in FR-3, FR-7 or FR-2. Turn "flagged" into a testable rule, e.g. "every AI-suggested Activity and tip is checked against recorded Restrictions and Strategies before display. Items that can't be confirmed as general engagement techniques show 'check with your therapist'. A fixed evaluation set of N cases passes before the pilot."
- **[medium]** Soft adjectives without bounds (FR-3 "sensible default list", FR-14 "sensible defaults pre-filled", FR-13 "Gentle reminders" and "a few Activities", FR-10 and UJ-1 "gentle trend" / "gentle progression", FR-16 "too few to show a trend", FR-9 "similar Activities") — *Fix:* name the default Status Vocabulary (the addendum already has it), the default reminder cadence and time, the Plan size (e.g. 3–5 Activities a week), the minimum Observation count for showing a trend, and what "similar" means (same Goal, same routine).
- **[medium]** Several FRs are under-specified for stories (FR-6, FR-8, FR-10 Guided mode, FR-13) — FR-6 doesn't say what "carried over" does to the Goal and its Observations: same Goal record in a new period, or a new linked Goal? Guided mode has no consequences at all. FR-13 doesn't say whether the suggested Plan is AI-generated or rule-based, or when a week starts. FR-8 has no consequence for hide or favourite (does hiding exclude the Activity from FR-9 and FR-13?). *Fix:* add 1–2 consequences to each, covering lineage data semantics, Guided mode step behaviour, the Plan generation source and week boundary, and hide/favourite effects.
- **[medium]** Offline scope is narrower than the flow needs (§6 Resilience "an Observation in progress is not lost") — The prep, activity, review flow also creates Marks, timer results and helpful-tip ratings during a session. The curated starter tips are the obvious offline fallback, but the PRD doesn't say they work offline. *Fix:* extend Resilience to cover Practice Session data (Marks, timer, Review), and state whether the starter Coach Tips and Prep cards are available offline.
- **[low]** Speed NFR target is ambiguous (§6 "from opening the app to a started Activity in ≤30 seconds") — It's unclear whether "started" means after Prep. FR-11 allows up to a minute for reading Prep. *Fix:* measure to "Prep card open" or "Activity started, excluding Prep reading time".
- **[low]** Starter tip content covers only one scenario (FR-12, addendum §D) — The seed tips cover shared reading only. FR-12 needs starter tips for four Marks ("Losing attention", "Upset", "Too easy", "Too hard") across activity types. *Fix:* note the minimum starter set (Marks × common routines) as a content deliverable.

## Scope honesty — adequate
The Non-Goals (§7) and Out of Scope lists (§8.2, FR-5, FR-15) do real work, including "No audio or video capture", "Multiple children per Family", and feeding as tagged notes only. The open-items density is very low: no `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, one `[NOTE FOR PM]`, and "There are no open questions." That's acceptable for a pilot, but it overclaims. One confirmed decision contradicts §8.2, and some real gaps are left for the reader to infer.

### Findings
- **[high]** Reminders decision contradicts the out-of-scope list (FR-13 "Gentle reminders are on by default", §10, vs §8.2 "push reminders beyond basic opt-in. Later", and addendum §B: iOS web push needs installation and permission) — Push can't be both on by default and opt-in, and on iOS it can't be on by default at all. UX and architecture will resolve this differently. *Fix:* state precisely what is on by default (e.g. "in-app Today nudge on by default; web push offered during onboarding after install, opt-in") and align §8.2 and §10 with it.
- **[medium]** Family membership lifecycle is missing (FR-1, §3 Caregiver "equal access", §5 "Caregivers can … delete all of their Family's data") — There's no way to remove a Caregiver or for one to leave. Any invited Caregiver, including one whose relationship with the Family has changed, could delete all Family data, with no confirmation or safeguard. *Fix:* add consequences to FR-1 covering removing or leaving the Family, and confirmation for Family-wide deletion. If the pilot accepts the risk, state that explicitly as a non-goal.
- **[medium]** "There are no open questions" hides inferable gaps (§10) — The real gaps are the document-PII handling, Restriction definition, Plan generation source, caregiver removal and pilot data terms raised above. *Fix:* reopen a short Open Questions list or resolve each gap in place. Low density is fine for a pilot, but a false "none" is not.
- **[low]** Unclear boundary for sensory scope (§8.2 "sensory profiles. Later." vs §3 Child Profile "sensitivities" and FR-15 "tagged notes for … sensory needs") — *Fix:* add one line saying v1 covers free-text sensitivities and tagged notes, and that structured sensory profiles are deferred.

## Downstream usability — adequate
This PRD sits at the top of a chain feeding UX, architecture and stories, so this dimension matters. The Glossary is strong and mostly followed. FR, UJ and SM IDs are contiguous, and every FR cross-reference resolves (see Mechanical notes). Two things will cause friction: one overloaded term ("setting"), and the absence of a first-run journey, which UX will have to invent.

### Findings
- **[medium]** "setting" is overloaded and has synonyms (FR-3 "setting such as clinic, school, or online" means where the Care Team Member works; FR-4, §3 Criterion and FR-14 "setting or routine" mean the practice context; FR-16 "settings or routines"; FR-17 "context patterns") — The data model will merge or split these unpredictably, and generalisation (FR-16) depends on the practice-context meaning. *Fix:* add Glossary entries, e.g. **Care Setting** for the Care Team Member's venue and **Context** for routine or setting in Goals and Observations, then use them consistently.
- **[medium]** No onboarding or first-run journey (§2.3, addendum §B "Onboarding should guide installation") — Creating a Family, setting up the Child Profile, installing the PWA, granting reminder permission, adding Care Team Members and first Goals, and inviting a co-caregiver are all prerequisites for UJ-1 to UJ-4. None is a journey. *Fix:* add a short UJ-0 (first run to first Practice Session), or at least an ordered list of onboarding steps in §4.1.
- **[low]** Uneven UJ structure (§2.3 UJ-2, UJ-3, UJ-4) — Only UJ-1 has an Entry state. UJ-4 has no Persona + context line. Unnamed composite protagonists are appropriate given the privacy rule. *Fix:* add Entry state and Persona + context lines so each UJ stands alone.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a consumer product with meaningful UX, and the PRD uses journeys where they carry weight. UJ-1 in particular (prep, phone away, Mark, Review) defines the core interaction better than the FRs do. The rigor suits a lean pilot PRD: no persona sprawl, a short NFR list, and solution detail correctly pushed to the addendum and labelled "proposals, not decisions". Its chain-top role raises the bar for downstream usability, covered above. No over-formalisation was found.

### Findings
- None beyond those above.

## Mechanical notes
- **ID continuity:** FR-1 to FR-17, UJ-1 to UJ-4, SM-1 to SM-5 and SM-C1 to SM-C2 are contiguous and unique.
- **Cross-references:** all FR cross-references resolve correctly: FR-2 → FR-9 and FR-12, FR-7 → FR-5, FR-10 → FR-14, FR-14 → FR-15, and every §10 and addendum reference. The one wrong mapping is SM-C2 "Counterbalances SM-5" (see Strategic coherence).
- **Assumptions Index:** there are no `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and no index, which is consistent with §10. There is one `[NOTE FOR PM]` (§5).
- **Terms used but not defined in the Glossary:**
  - "cue" (Prep cues). Its difference from Coach Tip is blurred: UJ-1 says "which tip or cue helped", FR-14 says "which Coach Tip helped".
  - "Guided mode"
  - "inventory" / "skill inventories" / "inventory list" (FR-14, FR-15, §8.1)
  - "restriction" (FR-9)
  - "domain" (FR-4, UJ-2)
  - "Today" (FR-13)
  - "Log a moment" (FR-14)
  - "goal sheet" (UJ-2)
  - "Care Team" used on its own ("Care Team Goals", "Care Team's Strategies"; only Care Team Member is defined)
- **Synonyms and drift:**
  - "session" / "Session length" (FR-12, FR-16, SM-C2) vs **Practice Session**
  - "attention span" (FR-10) vs "attention duration" (FR-10, FR-16) vs "how long the child attended" (UJ-1)
  - "practice consistency" (FR-16) vs "practice frequency" (FR-17)
  - "Profile fields" (FR-2) vs **Child Profile**
  - "prompt condition" (UJ-2) vs **Prompt Level** / **Criterion**
  - "quick log" (FR-10) vs **Review** / Quick Observation (FR-14)
  - lowercase "caregiver" in FR-15 vs **Caregiver**
- **Count mismatches:** cues are "2–3" in UJ-1 but "1–3" in FR-11. Tips are "at most 2–3" in UJ-1 but "2–3" in FR-12.
- **Lineage vocabulary:** the Glossary lists "*carried over from*, *revised from*, *retired*". FR-6 adds "*new*" and uses "*carried over*". UJ-2 uses "*revised*". Use one set everywhere.
- **Prompt Level example (§3):** the ordering "maximum cues" between physical cue and verbal + gestural cue, and "verbal prompt" below "model shown", is non-standard. It will likely seed the default editable list, so check or genericise it.
- **Section consistency:** §4.6 has no Description line, unlike §4.1 to §4.5. FR-12 repeats "Realizes UJ-1", which is already stated in §4.3.
- **Privacy scan:** no names, PII or wording that implies a specific real family were found. The PRD frames itself for "parents and caregivers" generally, and UJ personas are "illustrative composites". The only concern is the low-severity note on possibly transcribed examples (Substance over theater).
- **Required sections:** all are present for a lean consumer pilot PRD: Vision, Users, UJs, Glossary, FRs, Guardrails, NFRs, Non-Goals, MVP, SMs and Decisions.
